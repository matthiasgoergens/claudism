# The Quant Quarter: Q3 2026

This quarter's top stories in Asian quant were a whole team changing
firms and the market makers' expansion in Hong Kong.

### The Arrowpoint lift-out

On 13 August, Bloomberg reported that Arrowpoint Investment Partners, an
Asia-focused hedge fund manager, had hired the regional head and the
whole China investing team of O'Connor, the hedge fund business UBS sold
to Cantor Fitzgerald last year.

John Bradshaw, who ran O'Connor's Asia business for years and before
that worked in UBS's proprietary trading operation, joins Arrowpoint in
Singapore as deputy chief executive in late September. Tan Jia, who
headed O'Connor's China long-short strategy and came to O'Connor from
Och-Ziff, has already joined, with four members of his team.

The team managed about $1bn across a standalone strategy, institutional
separately managed accounts, and capital allocated from O'Connor's
global multi-strategy fund, working from Singapore, Hong Kong and
Shanghai. O'Connor as a whole managed about $11bn at the time of the
sale.

My read: a change of owner is a natural moment for a team to leave, and
this one left together, six people with their strategy and their
regional head. A team with a live track record is worth more to a buyer
than any single researcher, and I expect to see more teams move as
units.

### New in Hong Kong

US manager DLD Asset Management is opening in Hong Kong to expand its
relative-value trading across Asia. Its first senior hire there is Sohit
Khurana, co-founder of the Hong Kong firm Maso Capital Partners.

### Other moves

Jain Global hired Jon Stead, Morgan Stanley's head of high-yield trading
in London.

Citadel is rehiring Matt Giannini, from Walleye Capital, for his third
spell at the firm; he is expected to start in 2027.

Chris Drew, formerly of Jump Trading's digital-asset business, joined
Fasanara Digital as head of trading.

### Offices

On 5 September, Bloomberg reported that Jump Trading is taking another
11,000 square feet at One IFC in Hong Kong: a new floor and more of the
one it already occupies. It is hiring a medium-to-low-frequency
statistical arbitrage team, led by Zoey Zhao, formerly of Citadel
Securities, as well as people for AI roles. In May, Jump also took
99,305 square feet at 50 Hudson Yards in New York, doubling its office
space there.

Jane Street has reportedly grown to more than 110,000 square feet across
six floors in Hong Kong, and IMC is growing there too. The expansion
comes as Hong Kong's IPO market and trading volumes pick up.

### Revenues and flows

Citadel Securities' trading revenue for the first two quarters of 2026
adds up to $11.6bn, and its net income to $5.2bn. It is reported to
handle over a third of US retail stock trades. Jane Street and Hudson
River Trading also reported unusually large revenues.

In Singapore, Quantedge returned 34.6% in the year to July. It manages
$6bn to $7bn (reports differ) across roughly 300 markets, and has
averaged close to 20% a year since it launched in 2006. It is reported
to target 25-30% annualised volatility, well above what most
institutional investors accept.

In Goldman Sachs's survey of allocators, quant is the most sought-after
strategy for 2026: on net, 23% of allocators plan to increase their
exposure to it. Quant funds took over 70% of the industry's $78bn of net
inflows in 2025, and led inflows for the second year running.

### Into Q4

I expect more team lift-outs. Arrowpoint has shown that one can be done
in Asia.
